Press Release Sample Brussels Policy Framing

Fair Electrification Must Put Low-Income Households First as Europe Moves Beyond Fossil Heating

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE · Brussels · Target Media: EU Energy, Climate & Social Policy Desks

BRUSSELS — European policymakers must pair the upcoming EU Electrification Action Plan with targeted upfront financing if clean heating technologies are to shield households from volatile fossil gas prices, civil society and municipal energy experts warned ahead of today’s Brussels policy roundtable.

Across the European Union, millions of citizens still rely on imported fossil fuels to heat their homes. While electric heat pumps powered by domestic renewables offer a proven pathway to cut both emissions and household energy bills, high upfront installation costs and split incentives between landlords and tenants continue to slow uptake among lower-income households.

“Decarbonising heating is not just an engineering challenge—it is a test of democratic fairness,” said the programme lead ahead of the exchange. “Without social leasing models, targeted EU cohesion funding, and clear municipal one-stop shops, the clean transition risks leaving vulnerable residents locked into expensive fossil systems.”

Key Policy Recommendations for EU Institutions:

  • Bridge the upfront cost gap: Ring-fence revenues from the Social Climate Fund to cover up to 100% of installation costs for energy-poor households.
  • Rebalance electricity and gas taxation: Remove distortionary levies on clean electricity so heat pumps deliver immediate monthly savings over gas boilers.
  • Empower local authorities: Support municipal heat planning and citizen energy communities across urban and rural regions.

Media Advisory: Brussels Roundtable & Outreach Note

Format & Audience Strategy: Timed for morning Brussels media briefings (08:30 CET embargo lift) and pitched selectively to Brussels energy correspondents alongside regional consumer outlets. Follow-up briefing notes are tailored separately for Members of the European Parliament (ITRE / ENVI committees) and municipal housing networks.
